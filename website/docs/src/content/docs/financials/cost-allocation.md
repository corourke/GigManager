An invoice's lines show each item's price, but the total you paid also includes sales tax, shipping and fees. Those extras belong to the items too.

GigWrangler spreads them across the lines in proportion to price. The result is each item's **cost**, the amount it really cost you. It shows as **Unit Cost** on the review screen and **Cost** in the Purchases report.

## Example

| | Price | Share of $48 tax and shipping | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| XLR cable | $100 | $12 | **$112** |
| Amplifier | $300 | $36 | **$336** |
| **Invoice** | $400 | $48 | **$448** |

The invoice total was $448 and the prices add up to $400, so every item costs 12% more than its price.

## Why it matters

- **Equipment value:** the amplifier's cost is $336, not $300. That's the figure that counts for your records and your taxes.
- **Gig costs:** if the cable was for one gig, the gig's money out shows $112.
- **Totals that add up:** the costs always add up to the invoice total. When they don't, the review screen says **Mismatch**; check the invoice total and the lines before saving.

## Things to know

- **The spread only changes when you change the invoice:** a price, a quantity or the **Invoice Total**. Opening a saved purchase leaves its costs alone.
- **Purchases imported from a spreadsheet** often have a cost but no printed price. Their costs are kept as they are, and if you change the total they're scaled from their current costs.
- **Discounts** lower the invoice total, so they're spread across the items the same way.
- **Store credit** is a way of paying, not a discount. Enter the order's full total and note the credit under the payment method.
